Merchant Offer Optimization Agent

Proposed solution architecture, decision workflow and implementation approach for evidence-led merchant campaign optimization on Google Cloud.

1. What business problem are we solving?

A bank promotes merchant cashback campaigns. Some offers receive few redemptions, others decline suddenly, and some mostly reward customers who already shop there. A SQL rule flags the symptom, but there can be several explanations: restrictive offer terms, poor delivery, a shop closure, negative customer experience, or targeting mismatch.

MerchantSpice Route Kitchen
Offer10% capped ₹200
Minimum spend₹2,000
Budget₹40,000

R1 · Low redemption

6 of 150 = 4% distinct customers used the offer. POC alert if rate is below 5%.

R2 · Sudden drop

18% → 6% in comparable periods. Investigate closure, declining merchant activity, and exposure.

R3 · Low acquisition

13 of 15 = 87% redeemers are existing customers despite a new-customer objective.

Rules are sample configuration, not banking standards. Apply only when campaign is active, data is complete and the denominator is defined. An alert is an investigation trigger, not confirmed failure.

2. Why AI instead of only deterministic logic?

Code & SQL

What deterministic systems do best

Compute redemption rates; enforce eligibility; join records; identify spending ranges; calculate cashback exactly; reject budget violations. These remain normal Python and SQL.

AI agent

What is genuinely different

Interpret dated free-text reviews and merchant notes; select a useful next check based on findings; compare multiple hypotheses; propose allowed alternatives; explain uncertainty and trace evidence to source records.

Our claim is deliberately limited: An agent may reduce the amount of manual cross-system investigation. A fixed Python playbook is still a valid baseline. The POC should compare the two rather than assuming AI wins.

Six core BigQuery / optional local SQLite tables

These are the only six structured business tables needed for the initial POC. Each has a defined grain (what one row represents), primary key (PK), and foreign keys (FK). Open each table to see the schema and purpose.

Core BigQuery / optional local SQLite tables6
Unstructured CSV sources2
ML forecasting modelsNot modeled initially
merchants — One row per merchant

Columns: merchant_id PK; name; category; city; status

Why: Merchant identity and comparable-merchant grouping. Initial scope models one outlet per merchant.

Illustrative row: M001, Spice Route Kitchen, restaurant, Pune, active

campaigns — One row per campaign

Columns: campaign_id PK; merchant_id FK; objective; start_date; end_date; min_spend; cashback_pct; cashback_cap; budget; funding_type; max_redemptions_per_customer; status

Why: Approved campaign terms and objectives. Initial scope assumes terms remain unchanged during a live campaign.

Illustrative row: CMP131, M001, ACQUIRE_NEW, 2026-09-01..30, ₹2000, 10%, ₹200, ₹40000, bank, 1

customers — One row per fictional customer

Columns: customer_id PK; city; segment; marketing_eligible; card_status

Why: Audience attributes. Pseudonymous; no actual names, PANs, or sensitive characteristics.

Illustrative row: C001, Pune, new_to_merchant, true, active

offer_sends — One row per communication attempt

Columns: send_id PK; campaign_id FK; customer_id FK; sent_at; delivery_status; channel

Why: Who was targeted, who was successfully reached. Repeat sends allowed; count DISTINCT customers.

Illustrative row: S001, CMP131, C001, 2026-09-02, DELIVERED, app

transactions — One row per card payment

Columns: txn_id PK; customer_id FK; merchant_id FK; txn_ts; amount; txn_status

Why: ALL observed bank-card purchases, even when offer is not used. Only POSTED eligible payments count for replay.

Illustrative row: T001, C001, M001, 2026-09-10, ₹750, POSTED

offer_redemptions — One row per actual offer redemption

Columns: redemption_id PK; txn_id FK; campaign_id FK; customer_id FK; awarded_cashback; status; awarded_at

Why: Actual earned/awarded benefit. Must NOT infer redemptions merely from payments meeting threshold.

Illustrative row: R001, T105, CMP131, C006, ₹200, AWARDED, 2026-09-11

Two supporting text datasets

customer_reviews.csv

Fields
review_id, merchant_id, review_date, collected_at, rating, review_text, source_ref

Unstructured customer-experience evidence with dated source metadata. Illustrative reviews are synthetic.

merchant_feedback.csv

Fields
feedback_id, merchant_id, event_date, recorded_at, feedback_text, source_ref, verification_status

Merchant relationship notes: closure, staffing, chef changes, complaints. Claims are not automatically verified.

Two different kinds of information: customer reviews are customer opinions, while merchant feedback is a report from a restaurant representative. Neither alone proves a root cause. Always preserve a source ID and date.

Synthetic data design and quality requirements

Relationships and quality

  • All foreign keys must exist; stable synthetic IDs.
  • No real personally identifiable information or card numbers.
  • Transactions exist even when no offer redemption occurs.
  • Initial assumption: one active offer per merchant; no offer stacking.
  • One reward per eligible customer per campaign; rule is explicit.
  • Reversed/failed transactions must not count toward settled rewards.

Scenario diversity

  • Threshold friction without bad reviews.
  • Bad restaurant reviews with adequate offer terms.
  • Temporary closure and recovery.
  • Wrong-city targeting or poor delivery.
  • Mixed and contradictory evidence.
  • Tool failure, invalid AI proposal and budget rejection.
Deferred to later: dedicated alerts table, rule-version history, customer engagement events, merchant-wide footfall, approval tables, model registry, and experiment outcomes. JSON files and structured logs cover workflow metadata in the initial scope. If we later change terms mid-campaign, add effective-dated rules before replaying history.

Eight-step lifecycle

CODE exact rules & monetary checks · AI investigation choices & text understanding · HUMAN approval and operational decisions.

1

Prepare data and detect alerts

CODE

Load CSVs into BigQuery / optional local SQLite; quality-check IDs, dates, amounts; execute R1, R2, R3 in Python/SQL. Alert JSON contains campaign, period, rule, observed rate and threshold.

2

Load lightweight investigation policy

CODE

Read YAML: task objective, read-only tool catalog, allowed thresholds, cashback rates, budget ceiling, max tool calls and evidence requirements.

3

Fetch campaign baseline

AI CODE

Agent calls get_campaign_performance, get_campaign_terms, get_spend_distribution. SQL computes rates and bill distributions, not the LLM.

4

Choose follow-up leads

AI

Depending on initial evidence, request get_customer_reviews, get_merchant_feedback, get_delivery_metrics or get_comparable_campaigns. Avoid unnecessary tools.

5

Interpret and compare hypotheses

AI

Summarize review themes and merchant-reported incidents with source IDs. Separate verified facts, claims, possible explanations and missing evidence.

6

Generate candidate interventions

AI CODE

LLM suggests permitted alternatives (e.g. ₹1500/₹1750 thresholds, limited targeting). Policy validator rejects disallowed inputs BEFORE financial calculation.

7

Historical replay, compare and recommend

CODE AI

simulate_campaign_change recomputes eligibility and cashback on existing POSTED transactions. The LLM discusses likely trade-offs but cannot claim future uplift from replay.

8

Validate report and hand over

CODE HUMAN

Structured report contains evidence, calculation refs, assumptions, alternatives, risks and recommendation. Save trace JSON. No automatic campaign edit or customer contact.

Critical guardrails

Agent may

  • Query approved synthetic datasets through bounded tools.
  • Read dated reviews and merchant notes.
  • Suggest business-rule-compliant experiments.
  • Report uncertainty and missing evidence.

Agent must not

  • Edit offers, customer lists, or transactions.
  • Compute monetary figures in free text instead of a calculator.
  • Invent customer visits, uplift or restaurant footfall.
  • Promise future redemptions based on historical replay.
  • Claim poor reviews caused a decline without stronger proof.

End-to-end case walkthrough — 12 stages

End-to-end build walkthrough — Spice Route Kitchen

An end-to-end illustration, from source ingestion to an evidence-backed recommendation and auditable decision record. Use Next to walk the flow or scroll through the technical reference below.

POC assumptions: One merchant location; one campaign per merchant in period; one redemption per targeted customer; no offer stacking; no mid-campaign terms change; rewards capped at ₹200; historical replay only (no trained forecasting model).
STEP 1 OF 12 · ENGINEERING

1 — Prepare the source data

Create six BigQuery / optional local SQLite tables. Load synthetic merchant, customer, campaign, sends, transactions, redemption records plus two review/feedback CSVs. Validate primary/foreign keys, dates, valid statuses and one-redemption-per-customer assumptions.

Concrete example

Example: CMP131 references merchant M001, and all its reward records link to real payment IDs.

STEP 2 OF 12 · DETERMINISTIC

2 — Generate the alert

SQL counts 150 distinct targeted customers and six distinct targeted redeemers in September. Python calculates 6/150 = 4%; rule R1 fires because configured trigger is <5%. Emit JSON rather than writing an alerts table.

Concrete example

{"alert_id":"A001","campaign_id":"CMP131","rule_id":"R1","redemption_rate":0.04,"threshold":0.05,"status":"OPEN"}

STEP 3 OF 12 · ORCHESTRATION

3 — Start the agent

The Python app passes A001, available read-only tool descriptions, campaign policy and an instruction to report evidence and uncertainties. The LLM cannot query the DB directly: it must request a permitted tool.

Concrete example

Agent goal: investigate low usage and propose only allowable, evidence-backed steps.

STEP 4 OF 12 · AI SELECTS / SQL EXECUTES

4 — Fetch campaign details

Agent calls get_campaign_terms(CMP131), then get_campaign_performance(CMP131). Results: 10% cashback, capped ₹200, min bill ₹2000, one reward/customer, budget ₹40000; 150 targeted, 6 redeemed.

Concrete example

Tools return structured JSON with source table names and timestamps.

STEP 5 OF 12 · AI SELECTS / SQL EXECUTES

5 — Analyze spending patterns

Agent asks get_spend_distribution(M001). The tool returns merchant card payment buckets; example median ticket = ₹750. The agent forms a hypothesis: ₹2000 threshold might be restrictive. Median ≠ proof of causation.

Concrete example

Hypothesis H1: minimum spend may reduce redemption; request additional evidence.

STEP 6 OF 12 · AI + TEXT SOURCES

6 — Inspect other evidence

Agent checks dated customer_reviews.csv and merchant_feedback.csv. For this first case, suppose reviews are mostly positive and merchant notes do not report closure. This weakens an operational-disruption hypothesis but does not prove none exists.

Concrete example

Keep review IDs and feedback IDs beside every extracted theme.

STEP 7 OF 12 · AI SUGGESTS / RULES VALIDATE

7 — Generate alternatives

The AI proposes keeping ₹2000 as baseline, testing ₹1500, and testing ₹1000 on a controlled segment. validate_proposal checks a YAML allowlist and rejects any illegal cashback/budget setup.

Concrete example

Illustrative policy: allowed thresholds ₹1000, ₹1500, ₹2000; cashback fixed at 10%, cap ₹200.

STEP 8 OF 12 · DETERMINISTIC

8 — Run historical replay

Python re-evaluates existing eligible POSTED transactions for each candidate. Apply min-spend condition, 10% cashback, ₹200 cap, one reward per customer, and exclude failed/reversed payments. Return retrospective counts and cost.

Concrete example

Replay does NOT predict who will visit next month or incremental profit.

STEP 9 OF 12 · DETERMINISTIC

9 — Check budget with a safe bound

For this campaign, at most 150 eligible targeted customers can each receive one capped ₹200 benefit. The theoretical upper bound is ₹30000, below the ₹40000 budget. This only holds with those explicit restrictions and no other spend against the budget.

Concrete example

150 × ₹200 = ₹30000 maximum under assumed one-reward-per-targeted-customer rule.

STEP 10 OF 12 · AI SYNTHESIS

10 — Compare options without false forecasts

The agent compares replay outputs and notes the difference between more historical qualifying purchases and truly incremental spend. No model yet estimates future redemption. The agent may choose TEST rather than CHANGE.

Concrete example

Possible conclusion: small, approved experiment worth considering; expected uplift UNKNOWN.

STEP 11 OF 12 · AI + CODE VALIDATION

11 — Produce structured report

Return JSON: alert, observations, evidence IDs, hypotheses, rejected candidates, replay references, assumptions, missing data, recommendation and status. Validate schema and supported facts in Python.

Concrete example

Status = TEST. Do not call a hypothesis a verified root cause.

STEP 12 OF 12 · ENGINEERING + HUMAN

12 — Log, review, and evaluate

Write JSONL tool-call trace with inputs, outputs or references, timings/errors, model and prompt version. Show report in CLI or Streamlit. A human approves any eventual campaign change. Later the generic eval framework reads these traces.

Concrete example

Evaluate tool choice, correct SQL-derived facts, review grounding, budget policy and unsafe assertions.
